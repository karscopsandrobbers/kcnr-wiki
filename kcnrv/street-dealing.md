Carry drugs or guns and you can work a spot: stand on a street corner and sell to the people who come to you, one at
a time. It is slower than selling to a gang's corner by the box, but it pays the full street price.

## Working a spot

There are dozens of spots on street corners all over the map, away from the gangs' own corners (their dealers work
those). You find them on foot.
Walk past one with drugs or a gun in your pockets and press <kbd>E</kbd> on **Deal on this corner**. A spot that has run
dry for the day says so instead.

**A spot's name.** Every clean sale at a spot makes it better known, for everybody who works it after you: a well-known
spot gets customers sooner and more of them before it runs dry. Left alone, it is slowly forgotten. You'll notice it
in how quickly they come. A bust burns it:
sell to an undercover officer there, get seen selling there by an officer, or get arrested working it, and nobody buys
there for a week. Its prompt says so.

**Hot spots.** Four spots a day are on everybody's map, two in the city and two out in Blaine County, all well apart.
They pay more. A new four every day at midnight UTC.

While you work, bars at the bottom right show:

- **Demand**: how much the spot has left today. Each sale uses some up, and a dry spot fills back up over a few hours.
- **Turf**: whose ground you're on and what that gang thinks of you.
- **Prices**: what each thing you carry sells for at your price right now, a dose or a gun.
- **Takings**: what you've made here.

The keys show at the bottom right, and work anywhere around the spot: <kbd>+</kbd> and <kbd>-</kbd> move your price,
<kbd>H</kbd> strikes a pose while you wait and changes it on the next press (smoking, hanging out, leaning, wary and more),
held it stops posing, and <kbd>Backspace</kbd> stops working. All of them can be changed in your FiveM key bindings. Stay close to the spot, on foot.
Walk off, get in a vehicle, run out of things to sell, or let the spot run dry, and you stop, and so does your pose.

## Customers

Somebody turns up every half a minute to a minute, walking up from down the street, or now and then out of a shop or a
house nearby; while they're out of view, an arrow at the edge of your screen points to them. They stop in front of you
and say what they want and what they'll pay. Done with you, they head back the way they came.

- <kbd>E</kbd> **Sell**: you hand it over and they hand you the cash, right there in the street for anyone to see.
- <kbd>G</kbd> **Send them away.**
- Leave them too long and they give up and go.

**Drugs.** A customer wants one to three doses of any drug: most often something you carry, sometimes something you
don't, and then they move on. They pay the drug's full street price by its purity, the same as a gang's dealer
charges for a bag.

**Guns.** Somebody carrying guns gets gun buyers: one gun each, at **a quarter of Ammu-Nation's price**. Carry both
and some customers want a gun. They're more common in the rough parts of town (Davis, Strawberry, Chamberlain Hills,
Rancho, Cypress Flats, El Burro Heights, La Mesa, Elysian Island, Sandy Shores, Grapeseed, Stab City) and where the local
gang is at war, and rarer in the city.

**Your price.** <kbd>+</kbd> raises your price and <kbd>-</kbd> lowers it, from 20% under the street price
to 20% over. Cheaper brings customers sooner; dearer, and they're slower to come. Set it between customers: once one
is on the way, it stays as it is.

## Rival dealers

Now and then, while you work a spot, a gang's dealer sets up a little way off and starts taking your customers: the
busier the spot, the likelier. He takes about half of them, more if you're dearer than him and fewer if you're cheaper.
He shows as a red dot on your map, and a red arrow at the edge of your screen points to him while he's out of view.
Aim a gun or a blade at him to run him off (his gang won't like it, and now and then he pulls a gun and fights for the
spot), or walk up and press <kbd>G</kbd> **Pay him to leave** for the day (the money goes to his gang). Kill him and
you've killed one of theirs, but his stash of a few bags is on the ground.

Another player working a spot close to yours is a rival too: each customer goes to one of you, mostly the cheaper.

## The gangs' ground

Most rough streets are some gang's ground (see [Gangs and street crime](../gangs-and-street-crime/)).

- **A gang that counts you a friend** takes **a fifth** of every sale on its ground.
- **Anybody else** dealing there pays no cut, but the gang thinks a little less of you with every sale, and a gang
  that turns hostile comes for you the way it always does.

## The risks

- **An officer who sees a sale**: **Drug Trafficking**, or **Weapons Trafficking** for a gun, 2 stars.
- **Witnesses.** Somebody who watched the deal go down can phone it in, and you get the same 2 stars and the police are
  told. Only someone who actually saw it can. Wear a mask and they can't say who you are.
- **Undercover officers.** Now and then a customer is a cop. Sell to one and they take the goods, pull a gun on you,
  and you're wanted at once with the police on their way. There's no telling them apart.
- **Bad deals.** Now and then a customer pulls a knife on you instead, or once in a while a pistol. <kbd>E</kbd> hands over half your
  cash, <kbd>G</kbd> half of each drug you carry (a gun if you have no drugs), or fight them off. Don't take long: they
  go for you in seconds. They're robbing because they're broke, so most have nothing on them. Robbers are more likely in the rough parts of town, at night, when you're carrying a lot of cash, and when
  you're selling cheap.

## Junkies

Junkies hang around the gangs' corners, slumped against a wall or smoking. Working a spot near them, one may come over
instead of a stranger. They want one dose of whatever you have and offer little for it: <kbd>Y</kbd> **Ask for more**,
and either they find a bit more or they walk off.

You don't need to be working a spot. Walk up to a junkie with drugs on you: <kbd>E</kbd> **Sell a dose** sells them one of
your cheapest drug for what little they have (a junkie only has money now and then), <kbd>G</kbd> **Give a dose** hands it
over free. Given one, they're likelier to overdose.

A gang that watches you put down a junkie, a customer or a robber laughs it off. Kill one of theirs and it's a fight.

Now and then a junkie overdoses: they writhe on the ground, and stay down even if you walk into them, with
**Overdosing** over them in red that you can see from well off. Walk up and press <kbd>E</kbd> **Call a medic**: it goes to the medics on duty as a real call. The first to take it gets the patient, and you get thanked (and a little XP, once in a while). If
no medic is on duty, or nobody comes in time, they die there.

## Stashes

Keep product off your body, where a frisk can't find it: hide it in a dumpster or a bin. Stand at one and press
<kbd>G</kbd> **Hide something here**, and pick what goes in and how much. Your stash there shows as **Your stash** when you
come back; take out what you need. You can have three stashes, a bag's worth in each. A stash takes drugs, weapons,
rounds, valuables, masks and tools; not fishing gear or bait, food, clothes or bags, so with none of those on you a bin
only offers a search. While a customer is in front of you, the bin's keys are put away.

Nobody else is told where they are, but they aren't safe: somebody searching that bin now and then finds your stash and
keeps it, and a police dog sent at the bin sniffs out drugs or guns in it, and the officer seizes them. Either way you
find out it's gone. Nobody can be charged for a stash nobody is standing at.

## Corners

Every gang corner deals drugs. Those in the rough parts of town, and the Kkangpae's in Little Seoul and Alta, deal guns
too: they buy boxes of product and crates of guns and rounds, and sell their friends both. See [Dealing with a corner](../gangs-and-street-crime/).

## Your record

The stat cards on the <kbd>F10</kbd> dashboard count street customers served, guns sold on the street and what they
made, the times you were robbed, robbers you put down, sales to undercover officers, and medics called for a junkie.
Doses sold count with your [drug stats](../drugs/), and the money made selling drugs on the street has a leaderboard.
